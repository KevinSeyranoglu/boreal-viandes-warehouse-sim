# Closed-loop test of the labour model

Generated by `python ml/closed_loop.py`: never edit the numbers by hand. For each seed the model is trained on the first 2/3 of the baseline run and plans pickers for the last 60 days. The warehouse is then simulated again with that plan (picker shifts set by it, every other role unchanged) and the same days are compared with the baseline run of the same seed. 5 seeds; the interval is the 95 % t interval of the paired difference.

## Does the forecast add value?

Most of the gain comes from one finding a shift report would also show: the evening shift had 18 pickers for about 5 % of the picking. The no-ML comparison keeps the same shift curves but uses one rebalanced crew per shift every day, sized from the training average (no forecast).

| Test days, per day | Fixed crew (baseline) | Plan (simulated) | Difference | 95 % interval | Plan better in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Picker-hours / day | 351 h | 227 h | **−124 h** | −132 h to −117 h | 5 of 5 |
| Wages / day | $16,420 | $13,176 | **−$3,244** | −$3,473 to −$3,014 | 5 of 5 |
| Late fines and expedites / day | $1,785 | $2,585 | +$800 | −$402 to +$2,001 | 1 of 5 |
| On-time | 99.3 % | 98.0 % | **−1.4 pts** | −2.6 pts to −0.1 pts | 0 of 5 |
| Profit / day | $1,371 | −$2 | −$1,373 | −$7,267 to +$4,521 | 3 of 5 |

Same picker-hours, but the forecast puts them on the days that need them: the per-shift plan saves **$1,054 a day more** than the static crew (−$292 to +$2,400) and keeps **+1.7 pts** more orders on time (+0.2 pts to +3.2 pts), paired by seed.
