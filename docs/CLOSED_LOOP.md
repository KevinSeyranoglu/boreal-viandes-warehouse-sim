# Closed-loop test of the labour model

Generated by `python ml/closed_loop.py`: never edit the numbers by hand. For each seed the labour model is trained on the first 2/3 of the baseline run and plans picker-hours for the last 60 days. The warehouse is then simulated again with that plan (picker shifts scaled to it, every other role unchanged) and the same days are compared with the baseline run of the same seed. 5 seeds; the interval is the 95 % t interval of the paired difference.

| Test days, per day | Fixed crew (baseline) | ML plan (simulated) | Difference | 95 % interval | Plan better in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Picker-hours / day | 351 h | 301 h | **−50 h** | −63 h to −37 h | 5 of 5 |
| Wages / day | $16,420 | $15,106 | **−$1,314** | −$1,694 to −$934 | 5 of 5 |
| Late fines and expedites / day | $1,785 | $3,885 | **+$2,100** | +$656 to +$3,544 | 0 of 5 |
| On-time | 99.3 % | 95.4 % | **−3.9 pts** | −5.8 pts to −2.0 pts | 0 of 5 |
| Profit / day | $1,371 | −$1,750 | −$3,120 | −$6,391 to +$150 | 1 of 5 |

The model promised a saving of **$815 ± 119 a day** (wages saved minus the late fines its capacity curve expects). Simulated, wages plus fines (the two lines staffing drives) changed by **+$786 a day**, and profit by −$3,120 (profit also carries spoilage and revenue noise between runs).
